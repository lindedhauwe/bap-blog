## What did we do?

We went to Frederic for a visual design consultation and showed him our first style board. You can read more about our styleboard in [developing the visual style](../2026-11-11-style-initiation/).

He told us the visual style is good and a strong start. Now we can refine it and think about how we are going to bring our idea to life visually.

We also talked about the screen. Until now, we planned to use a projection. Frederic warned us that the building is too brightly lit for a projection to look bright and high quality enough. Instead, he recommended an LED screen from Aluvision or a really big TV.

He also gave us two more tips:

- Email Klaus. He used LED screens at BUMP a few years ago and can tell us more about the price and practical details.
- Build a real frame around the screen, so it fits the paper-theatre theme as well.

## What did we learn?

We learned that our visual style works, but that the display matters just as much as the visuals. The best artwork won't convince anyone if it looks dull on a washed-out projection in a bright building.

## Why this direction?

Frederic's advice comes from experience with the same kind of setting, so we trust it. Switching to an LED screen keeps our visuals bright and polished. A frame around the screen turns the installation itself into a small theatre, which makes the installation more immersive.

## Next steps

- Email Klaus to ask about his experience with LED screens, and about the price.
- Look into LED screens from Aluvision and big TVs, and compare them.
- Think about what a frame around the screen could look like.
- Refine our style board and start thinking about how we will visualise our idea.
