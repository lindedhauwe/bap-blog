## What did we do?

For the fantasy genre we wanted more action than in the other minigames. In this scene a witch attacks the players, who work together to defeat her: player 1 attacks, and player 2 defends and can attack too, by bouncing the witch's spells back at her. We designed all the assets for this scene, and, like in the other genres, every asset is a reference to a well-known book or story.

### The witch

The villain is the witch from Roald Dahl's "De heksen". She has a life bar, which the players can slowly drain. The players don't have one, so they always win in the end. We drew her in four poses: two different attack poses, a hurt pose and an idle pose (from left to right).

![The witch in her four poses](./witch-poses.png)

![Reference for the witch's poses](./witch-poses-reference01.png)

### The controllers

Each player gets a tool that fits their role. Player 1 attacks with a wand, a reference to Harry Potter. Player 2 defends with an umbrella from Mary Poppins, which can be opened and closed. The umbrella can also bounce the witch's attacks back at her.

## Why this direction?

A witch who attacks and a player who can fight back makes this the most active genre, so it balances the calmer detective and history games. Splitting the roles into attacker and defender keeps both players busy and forces them to cooperate. Letting the umbrella bounce attacks back gives the defender an exciting role too, so player 2 isn't just waiting. Taking away the players' life bar means the game never ends in a loss, which keeps it fun for our young target audience. Building every asset on a reference from a known book keeps the scene recognisable and connects the game back to reading.
