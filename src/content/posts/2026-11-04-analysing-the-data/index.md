> **Good to know:** the client let us know the installation can't be too large-scale. There's no fixed budget per installation yet, but €5,000 is a good indication. In their words: "Boektopia tries to create as much impact as possible for as little budget as possible. As for space: as you'll have seen, our halls are already quite full, so a visible but compact installation is the way to go."

## What did we do?

On Tuesday 3 and Wednesday 4 November we analysed the data from our [Boektopia visit](../2026-11-03-boektopia-visit-interviews/). We held 12 interviews with parents and children aged 6 to 12, on Monday 2 November.

First, we put all the answers in one table, made charts per question and wrote down our own remarks. Then we compared the interviews with our [online survey](../2026-10-29-survey-results/), which 12 families filled in before the fair (28 October to 1 November). For that comparison, we asked Claude to analyse both surveys together with our observations, and we commented on its analysis.

## What did the interviews tell us?

### Funny and adventurous

Funny scored highest, followed by adventure. Most children combined the two. Nobody picked scary.

We have to take the genres with a pinch of salt. One child who loves Geronimo Stilton called it adventure, and only a little bit fantasy because it's about a talking mouse. After a few interviews we noticed that some genres weren't clear to children.

### Every child likes reading

Some answers in these charts are very specific, because we wrote down what people literally said. Those really belong to a broader answer. "Mostly comics" and "Son comics, daughter books" still mean the children like to read, so in practice all 12 answers are a yes.

Of all the families we asked, nobody said their child doesn't like reading. Only once did a parent say their child should read more books instead of comics, and even then it was about reading more books, because the child already read a lot of comics. (That came from the child; the mum sounded happy with how much they read.)

Since every child we asked likes reading, maybe our job isn't to *convince* children to read, which our first idea assumed. It's more to help them **discover more genres**.

Here too, the specific answers add up: "With brothers and sisters" and "Youngest alone, older ones together" are also about playing together. So 9 of the 11 children like to play with others, and only 1 prefers to play alone.

Most children like to play together, and who with depends on the child. We often heard mum and/or dad, but also brothers and sisters.

Many parents would join in organised activities, but not in free play, like in the play hall. That seemed obvious to us. It's not a clear majority, so an installation has to work both for parents who join in and for parents who'd rather watch.

### Waiting time

On average, an acceptable waiting time is around **10 to 15 minutes**, with 20 minutes as the upper limit ("10 minutes is fine, more than 20 isn't"). Some were willing to wait longer, but they were a minority. One family had skipped an activity last year because of the queue.

Children's answers were much more extreme, from "not wanting to wait" to 30 minutes. Some questions, like how long they would wait for an activity, were a bit unclear for children: they don't have a full sense of time yet. That's why the answers of children and parents differed.

### Other remarks

- **Making a comic** was mentioned twice as the best thing so far, once with the note "proud". That matches what we saw: children were proud of their goodie. Taking something home works.
- **Treasure hunts** were mentioned most often (4 times), both as something they had done and something they still planned to do. One child wanted to do a treasure hunt, "but not the bouncy castle". For children from about 6, searching and discovering is more attractive than just running around.
- One family didn't know the read-aloud session needed a **reservation**, and did something else. A drop-in installation, where you can just walk in, fits better.
- In general, the parents were all very kind and helpful.

## Comparing it with the online survey

The online survey added things we hadn't asked live: children want to **find a treasure** (10/12) and **defeat a monster** (8/12). Parents want to **do a task together with their child** (9/12) and to be surprised by something funny (8/12).

Almost everyone picked an experience of 5 minutes at most (11/12).

Both surveys point in the same direction: adventure and humour, searching for something, playing together and keeping it short. The biggest difference is comics: live they played a big role, online hardly at all.

The time questions weren't the same: live we asked about **waiting time**, online about how long the **experience** itself may take. Read together: an experience of 3 to 5 minutes, with a queue of no more than 10 to 15 minutes. People are willing to wait longer than they play.

Fantasy and magic came up more often online than at Boektopia, so it can definitely still be interesting.

## What did we learn?

Our conclusions for the design:

- **Adventure and humour** definitely go into the mini-games. Open question: is humour a genre of its own, or part of every genre?
- **Keep waiting times as short as possible**, around 5 to 10 minutes, with an experience of about 5 minutes. That means several people at the same time, instead of one person taking a long time.
- **Families prefer playing together over playing against each other.** Everyone has to be able to take part actively, parent and child together. Parents who prefer to watch should be able to do that comfortably too, and young children don't dare to do it alone in public, so a parent has to be able to join.
- **Make sure the game also works with fewer players** than we planned for.
- **The space will be limited**, so large installations are ruled out. A small, visible drop-in, like the colouring pallet, without a reservation.
- **Mini-games are still interesting** for our target group: about 5 minutes, with a goal.
- **Include comics in the recommendations** at the end.
- **Something to take home:** could we think about a small goodie? If our installation has to be smaller, a goodie can still leave a lasting impression.
