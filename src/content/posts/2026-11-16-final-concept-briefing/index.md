## What did we do?

We pulled everything we had worked on so far into one document: the official briefing of our concept, **The Land of Kaboeko**.

We started from our own basis: **who, why, how and what**, written down in Dutch.

![Our basis for the briefing on a yellow board: who (families with children aged 8 to 12 who visit Boektopia), why (encourage children to read more and discover books and genres), how (an interactive game they play themselves at Boektopia) and what (The Land of Kaboeko, where two players discover genres through minigames and leave with a mission to find books and complete their stamp card)](./golden-circle.jpg)

Then we gave Claude (AI) all our information, from the client's briefing and our research to the minigames, our style and the technical research, and asked it to draft a briefing. We read that draft critically, wrote our comments on yellow sticky notes, and made a new version. Versions 1 and 2 were drafts; after three rounds, version 3 became our **official briefing**, a 15-page PDF.

## The final briefing in short

### Summary

The Land of Kaboeko is an interactive game at Boektopia in which a parent and child discover five book genres together through short 1–2 minute minigames. Each minigame ends with book recommendations from that genre and a mission at the fair: find those books and complete your stamp card. Reading stops being a chore and becomes an adventure you share, one that continues after the game.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | Families at Boektopia: parents with children aged 8 to 12. |
| **Why** | Encourage children to read more and discover new books and genres. |
| **How** | An interactive game that parent and child play themselves, together, at Boektopia. |
| **What** | The Land of Kaboeko: two players discover genres through minigames and leave with a small mission: find books from the genre they just played and complete their stamp card. |

**How might we** combine play and reading, so families with children aged 8 to 12 at Boektopia can step into different literary genres, discover which genre suits the children best, and leave with a positive experience that makes them want to read?

### Research foundation

The briefing builds on our research into playful learning, the psychology of play, inclusivity, sustainability and the needs of our audience. A few of the insights and what they mean for the design:

- Children of 8 to 12 want to feel competent and like games with **rules and a real challenge**, so the game has a clear goal and isn't childish.
- **Movement** improves attention, but a physical action only helps when it **matches the story**: every button or movement means something, like steering a ship or opening an umbrella.
- The biggest risk for frustration is children not understanding what to do, so we explain through **visuals and a narrator, not text**.
- **Curiosity works as a reward**, so we use cliffhangers and surprises instead of points.
- Two children playing side by side isn't enough: the duo has to **make decisions together**, with shared final actions.
- **Inclusivity:** every clue in image, sound and text; large controllers usable with one hand; every movement has an alternative; no sudden flashes or loud noises.
- **Sustainability:** honeycomb cardboard for the booth, rented LED tiles, wired controllers that are reused edition after edition, and also recommending the library and second-hand books.

### The experience

A visit takes a family from a busy fair floor to a book in their hands:

1. **Notice:** the booth stands out with light, sound and laughing families.
2. **Watch:** waiting families see others play on the big screen.
3. **Choose:** in the queue, parent and child discuss which genre to play.
4. **Start:** five floating books show the genres, the players pick one and a countdown starts.
5. **Play:** a 1–2 minute minigame, each player with their own task.
6. **Finish:** a shared final action and a finale animation, where everyone succeeds.
7. **Recommendations:** books from that genre appear, matched to the child's age.
8. **Stamp card:** the family takes a card ("My ultimate genre is …") and fills it in.
9. **Mission:** with the card and the floor plan, they look for the genre's books at the fair and stamp the card.
10. **Discover:** the child finds a book to take home and keeps the card as a souvenir.

### The five minigames

Each genre is one minigame of 1–2 minutes with one clear goal, two different but equally fun roles, and a shared final action. Every minigame uses characters, objects and spells from well-known books, so the game leads straight to real books.

| Genre | Minigame | Player 1 | Player 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Adventure | **Storm at Sea:** sail through the storm to Treasure Island | Captain, steers with a wheel | Sailor, scoops water overboard |
| Fantasy & magic | **The Grand High Witch:** stop the witch | Wizard, casts spells with a magic staff | Protector, opens the umbrella when the witches attack |
| History | **The Great Dig:** dig up five treasures from the past | Digger, with a shovel | Archaeologist, brushes the finds clean |
| Comedy | **Make Them Laugh:** make five grumpy characters laugh | Tickler, with a big feather | Thrower, with a soft foam pie |
| Mystery | **The Stolen Book:** find the clues and catch the thief | Detective, with a magnifying glass | Catcher, with a net |

Design rules for every minigame: no game over and always a positive ending, the tasks depend on each other, and synchronised actions allow about one second of margin.

### Stamp card and mission

The stamp card carries the experience from the screen to the books. The child fills in "My ultimate genre is …", looks for a book from that genre at the fair and stamps the card with one of five **round self-inking stamps**, each with its own design and ink colour, placed next to the books of that genre. One card holds one stamp: playing another genre means a new card. Afterwards the card is a souvenir and a reading list.

### Look and feel

The Land of Kaboeko looks like a **paper pop-up book come to life**: a 2.5D layered paper cut-out style with white die-cut outlines and paper textures, Oswald and Andika as fonts, and a palette of white, blues, soft pink, coral and navy. Each player has their own colour. The booth uses the same style: a docking station shaped like a stack of books, and a honeycomb cardboard frame around the screen.

### Installation and technology

Everything is **wired**: reliable in a crowded hall, no batteries, no pairing. Each player has a controller read by an ESP32-S3, and one computer runs the Phaser game offline and drives the screen: an Aluvision LED wall of about 3 × 2 m for the client, and a TV or projector for the jury.

### Priorities (MoSCoW)

| Priority | Items |
| --- | --- |
| **Must have** | Screen + controllers · at least 3 genre games · sturdy, safe props · understandable without explanation · short play sessions · 2 players |
| **Should have** | 5 genre games · a stamp card · references to well-known books and characters · booth decorated in our style |
| **Could have** | Sound that enhances the experience · adjustable difficulty · personalised card · a random genre button |
| **Won't have** | More than 2 players · minigames longer than 3 minutes · projection as the final screen · scores or leaderboards · login or personal data · a game based on one specific book |

### Success criteria

The installation succeeds when families play it **without explanation**, finish it **with a smile**, and **walk to a book**. During the fair we can measure that: whether families understand the game on their own, whether every minigame ends within 2 minutes, whether families walk to the books and stamp their card, and whether the waiting time stays under 10–15 minutes.

## What are we still working on?

The briefing is official, but a few things are still open:

- **Aluvision:** we're in contact about Belgian rental partners and a price estimate for a 3 × 2 m wall for 2–3 weeks on two locations. We expect a price calculation this week, and we've asked to visit their showroom.
- **Budget:** we're calculating all costs for both controller options (Wii Nunchuk and a separate motion sensor).
- **Controllers:** the test parts are ordered; testing starts at the end of this week.
- **Well-known books:** we still have to check with the publishers which characters and quotes we may use.
- **Jury demo:** we prefer a large TV with our own laptop, to be discussed with our coach.
- **Stamps:** first design the five stamps and calculate the cost, then order them.
