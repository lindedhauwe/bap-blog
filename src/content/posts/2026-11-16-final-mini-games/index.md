## The client's feedback

When we presented our research and first game ideas to Boektopia, the [feedback](../2026-11-08-client-feedback/) on our target group was positive:

> "I think the research is spot on, these are exactly the kind of people we want to get interested in books. What isn't entirely clear to me is how the games link back to books. Video games that are vaguely related to books by means of genre is not enough of a link for me."

This was an important moment for us. Our games were fun and fitted their genre, but a game about "fantasy" or "adventure" could just as easily be linked to films or other video games. If players can't recognise the books in the game, it doesn't help Boektopia reach its goal: getting families (back) into reading.

## What we changed

We reworked every game so that the books themselves become the building blocks. Every villain, prop, spell, setting or character now comes from an existing book. We set a few rules for ourselves:

- **Mix books instead of using just one.** Each game combines elements from several books, so it never becomes a game about one specific title, and players discover more than one book.
- **The book, not the film.** We checked whether each reference comes from the book or from a film adaptation. In *Pinocchio*, for example, the sea monster is a giant shark in the book (the whale comes from the Disney film). Where possible, we draw characters as the books describe them.
- **Show the title.** Whenever a book element appears, the title of the book is shown on screen, so players learn where it comes from while they play.
- **End with the books.** Every game ends with a recommendation of the books that appeared in it, followed by the card "My ultimate genre is…" and a stamp route to the book stands at Boektopia.

## Our five final games

Each game follows the same structure: two players with their own role and prop, a short intro, rounds that build up in difficulty, a final action done together, and the book recommendations. Every game lasts a maximum of 3 minutes.

### 🌊 Adventure: Storm at sea

| | Player 1: Captain | Player 2: Sailor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prop** | A ship's wheel, fixed to the stand | A scoop |
| **Action** | Steer left or right to avoid the rocks | Scoop water out of the boat |

The players sail towards a treasure island. The sea starts calm, turns into a storm, and ends in a heavy storm with rocks everywhere and fish flying into the boat. When the storm settles, the ship washes up on an island, and the players dig up a treasure chest with a book inside.

**Book references:**

- The crocodile with the ticking clock, and Captain Hook's ship in the distance (*Peter Pan*)
- A mermaid who shows the way (*The Little Mermaid*)
- A giant shark swimming by (*Pinocchio*)
- The white whale making a huge wave (*Moby Dick*)
- A giant squid grabbing at the boat (*Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea*)
- The island and the treasure chest (*Treasure Island*, *Robinson Crusoe*)

### 🧙 Fantasy: Defeat the witches

| | Player 1: Wizard | Player 2: Protector |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prop** | A magic wand | The umbrella from *Mary Poppins* |
| **Action** | Perform spells from different books | Open the umbrella as a shield when the witches attack |

In a snowy forest with a lamppost (*The Chronicles of Narnia*), the Grand High Witch (*The Witches*) wants to turn all children into mice. The protector blocks her attacks, and the wizard fights back in three rounds:

1. **Gandalf's staff** (*The Hobbit*): raise the staff and strike down, and a soft flash of light blinds the witch.
2. **The Golden Cap** (*The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*): stand on your left foot ("Ep-pe, pep-pe, kak-ke!"), your right foot ("Hil-lo, hol-lo, hel-lo!") and both feet ("Ziz-zy, zuz-zy, zik!"), and the Winged Monkeys carry the witches away. This spell is in the book, but not in the famous film.
3. **Expelliarmus** (*Harry Potter*): the wand flies out of the witch's hand.

The witch flees and leaves a locked chest behind. Together, the players open it with "Open Sesame!" (*One Thousand and One Nights*). Inside are the recommended books.

### 🏺 History: The excavation

| | Player 1: Digger | Player 2: Archaeologist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prop** | A shovel | A brush |
| **Action** | Dig in the sand until you feel something hard | Brush the object clean and place it on the right board |

The players dig up five treasures from different periods and place them in the matching shapes on five wooden boards. In between, they sometimes find a wrong object, like a Lego brick, a dog poo or an old sock, and toss it away. The last find is huge: together, they brush it clean and discover an old golden book.

**Book references:**

- A dinosaur bone, prehistory (*Stig of the Dump*)
- A mouse's sarcophagus, Egypt (*Geronimo Stilton*)
- A Roman helmet (*Asterix*)
- The sword Excalibur, Middle Ages (*The Legend of King Arthur*, *De brief voor de koning*)
- A Viking helmet (*Wickie the Viking*, *How to Train Your Dragon*)

Each find comes with a short fun fact. For example, real Vikings never wore helmets with horns, but in many stories they do. That way children also learn the difference between a story and history.

### 😂 Humour: Tickle and splash

| | Player 1: Tickler | Player 2: Thrower |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prop** | A big feather | A soft foam water balloon or pie, attached to the stand |
| **Action** | Tickle a character | Make a throwing movement at a character |

Five grumpy characters stand on the screen. When they are tickled, they do a silly move. When they are hit, they shout their well-known catchphrase. Once every character has laughed, a giant whoopee cushion appears. The players jump on it until it explodes, and a book flies out.

**The characters:**

- Pippi lifts her horse above her head (*Pippi Longstocking*)
- Captain Underpants strikes a superhero pose (*Captain Underpants*)
- Rowley tries to dance cool but falls over (*Diary of a Wimpy Kid*)
- Lambik strikes a proud, puffed-up pose (*Suske en Wiske*)
- Dikkie Dik rolls over and wants to be petted (*Dikkie Dik*)
- Timon Verbeeck, Flemish children's author and YouTuber (*Project D.I.N.O.*)

### 🔍 Mystery: The stolen book

| | Player 1: Detective | Player 2: Catcher |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prop** | A magnifying glass | A net |
| **Action** | Search the screen for clues that only appear under the glass | Throw the net over the suspects running through the library |

A book has been stolen from the library. Player 1 finds three clues: muddy shoe prints, a black button and a moustache hair. Player 2 catches the four suspects. Then they compare the suspects with the clues and decide together who the thief is.

**The suspects:**

- The man in the bowler hat (*Emil and the Detectives*): the real thief, just like in the book
- Professor Moriarty (*Sherlock Holmes*): no moustache
- Captain Hook (*Peter Pan*): gold buttons instead of black ones
- The Big Bad Wolf (*Little Red Riding Hood*): paw prints, not shoe prints

Each clue rules out one suspect, so children really solve the case by reasoning. A wrong guess isn't a game over: the suspect gives a funny alibi and you try again.

## What we learned

The client's feedback made us realise that a genre alone doesn't link a game to reading. A link only works when players recognise something from a real book and are curious to find out more. By building every game out of real books, the game becomes a doorway to those books instead of a replacement for them.

We also noticed that this works for both target users. Children recognise characters like Captain Underpants or Harry Potter. Parents recognise the classics from their own childhood, like Pippi, Peter Pan and Sherlock Holmes.

## Next steps

- **Check the rights.** Many of these books are still protected by copyright. We will discuss with Boektopia which characters and elements we can use in a public installation. Using Timon Verbeeck also needs his permission, since he is a real person.
- **Test with the target group.** Do children recognise the books? Do they want to read them after playing?
- **Finalise the recommendations per genre**, with books that fit the age of the players.
